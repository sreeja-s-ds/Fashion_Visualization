# Fashion_Visualization

🧼🧮 **Fashion Product Dataset – Data Cleaning & Visualization**

✅ **Objective**
To gain meaningful business and customer insights through structured data preprocessing and visual analytics for fashion products.

📁 **Project Overview**
This project involves exploring, cleaning, and visualizing a fashion product dataset containing 1,000 entries and 9 features. The objective is to understand customer preferences, product trends, and pricing dynamics using Python libraries like Pandas, Matplotlib, and Seaborn.

📊 **Key Features**

🔍 Data Cleaning

📈 Data Visualization

📌 **Technologies Used**

📄Python (Pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn)

**Fashion Product Dataset: Visualization Report Analysis**

This report provides analysis and insights based on 15 visualizations created from a fashion product dataset containing 1,000 entries and 9 features.

---

**1. Distribution of Product Prices**
The histogram shows that most products are priced in the lower range, typically below $100. There's a long tail toward higher prices, indicating a few premium items.

**2. Top 5 Product Categories**
"Men's Fashion" and "Women's Fashion" dominate the dataset, suggesting that the platform or retailer caters mostly to general adult fashion rather than niche segments like kids or sports.

**3. Brands with Highest Average Ratings**
Brands such as Zara, Adidas, and H&M appear frequently with high average ratings, indicating consistent customer satisfaction and product quality among top brands.

**4. Price Variation by Product Size**
Boxplots reveal that sizes like XL and L have slightly higher median prices. This may be due to increased material use or popularity.

**5. Rating Distribution**
Ratings mostly range from 3 to 5, peaking around 4. This indicates generally positive customer sentiment, with very few low-rated products.

**6. Most Popular Product Colors**
Black, White, and Blue are the most common colors in the dataset. These colors likely have broader appeal and versatility in fashion.

**7. Price vs. Rating Relationship**
The scatter plot shows no strong correlation between price and rating. Both expensive and inexpensive items receive high ratings, suggesting customer satisfaction is not solely dependent on cost.

**8. Product Count per Brand**
A few brands dominate the product catalog (e.g., Adidas and H&M), while many others have fewer items listed. This imbalance could reflect strategic partnerships or brand popularity.

**9. Price Range by Category**
"Women's Fashion" tends to have a wider price range than "Men's Fashion," possibly due to more variety in product types and styles.

**10. Size Frequency**
Sizes M, L, and XL are most common, aligning with general population averages. Sizes like XS or XXL are relatively rare.

**11. Top Colors by Average Rating**
Neutral tones like Black and White have slightly higher average ratings. This may suggest customer preference for classic, versatile colors.

**12. Brand-wise Price Range**
Luxury or premium brands like Zara show higher price variance compared to mass-market brands, indicating a broader product spectrum.

**13. Product Name Frequency**
Items like Shoes, T-shirts, and Dresses are most frequently listed, suggesting focus on basic or essential fashion items.

**14. Average Rating by Product Name**
T-shirts and Dresses generally have higher ratings, suggesting they meet customer expectations more often compared to products like Accessories or Pants.

**15. Price Distribution by Color**
Colors like Red and Yellow show slightly higher price variability, likely due to their presence in both high-end and casual product lines.

---

**Conclusion:**
The dataset reflects a fashion catalog dominated by a few well-known brands and common product types. Prices vary by size and category, but do not significantly influence customer ratings. Popular colors and sizes align with general trends, and overall customer satisfaction appears high across the board.
